What this example proves—and what it doesn’t

This is my own Next to Kin / KinTag campaign for ACCA 2026 in Calgary, October 7–9. The supplied playbook and campaign summary describe 168 listings merged into 121 organisations, 20 letter variants for the top 10, a 12-page briefing, and 11 personal replies. The summary separates those replies from 10 auto-replies and 5 bounces.

These are documented preparation and reported reply counts, not independently audited results, booked meetings, sales or a conversion rate. The exhibit and sponsor research distinguishes listed people from likely contacts; it doesn’t establish who attended. Follow-up drafts are preparation, not proof of a completed sale.

Every event has different data and access limits. We agree on the scope first. Event outreach is quoted for the research, volume, materials and support involved; the site’s $999 AI implementation starting price is not a fixed price for a complete conference campaign.
