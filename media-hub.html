CASE STUDY / A MEDIA HUB

ONE DESK.
EVERY RECORDING.

A Regina team producing a library of spoken-word recordings. Different people, different rooms, different microphones. Every file still has to leave in one format, one naming pattern and one loudness. I’m keeping the client’s name out of it.

20days from a one-page pitch to a shared team workspace
38releases shipped, none failed
3roles: admin, producer, talent
0Google passwords handed to contributors

WHAT I BUILT

  • Media library. Drag in audio or video and it uploads straight to a shared Google Drive folder, sorted by date. Large files go up in pieces and retry on a bad connection.
  • Catalogue. Every piece gets a permanent ID, a stage from planned to delivered, and a version number.
  • Script library. Drafts, approvals and versions in one place. Talent confirm they’ve read the approved script.
  • A teleprompter that listens. It follows the reader’s actual words, counts down written pauses, and records each take as a backup.
  • Talent portal. Readers sign in, see only their own approved scripts, and upload recordings made at home.
  • Schedule, roster and procedures. Sessions with calendar export, a people list, and written procedures ready to print.

Nothing here is exotic. The work was getting four ordinary things to trust each other.

01 / THE BROWSER

The desk is a web page.

Audio is analysed and recorded on the reader’s own computer.

02 / SIGN-IN + SHARED RECORDS

Invited emails only.

Each role sees what it should. The catalogue, scripts and schedule stay in step for everyone.

03 / A SMALL BRIDGE SCRIPT

One file, one slot.

It checks who is signed in, then opens a single-file upload slot. Contributors never hold a Google password.

04 / GOOGLE DRIVE

Four folders.

Incoming, working, approved, delivery. Removing a file sends it to the trash for 30 days, not gone for good.

HOW IT DEVELOPED

  1. OCT 21A pitch you could listen to.One page with a before-and-after player and a loudness desk that processed a dropped file in the browser. No accounts, no storage.
  2. NOV 4A workspace you could click through.Six releases in one afternoon: schedule, media library, scripts and procedures. Everything saved in one browser only, behind a demo password.
  3. NOV 9The teleprompter.Adjustable pace, written pause cues and emphasis marks, shipped as four releases in one day.
  4. NOV 10, MORNINGThe teleprompter learns to listen.A microphone meter, then scrolling that follows the reader’s voice, then word-by-word tracking. A quiet USB microphone exposed a fixed trigger level, so the desk now measures the room and adapts.
  5. NOV 10, 2:35 TO 4:23 PMFrom demo to shared system.In under two hours: real team sign-in, a shared catalogue, uploads into Google Drive, remote uploads for talent, file removal, and the roster.
  6. NOV 10 TO 12Finishing audio in the hub.A vocal suite that cleans, evens out and sets the loudness of a whole file in the browser. In preview, not yet in the live version.

WHAT I CUSTOMIZED

  • Colours and type matched to the client’s own brand.
  • One fixed pattern for content IDs, file names and folders.
  • Pause and breath cues read straight from the way their scripts are already written.
  • Audio presets built around one producer’s voice chain and target loudness.
  • Microphone handling tuned to the USB recorder used in sessions.

01 / MY ROLE

I directed. AI helped build.

I set the requirements and checked the result. Claude Code wrote the implementation. That’s why most changes went live minutes after they were written.

02 / AI INSIDE

It listens. It doesn’t guess.

Speech recognition lets the teleprompter follow a reader word by word. The audio processing is conventional signal processing, chosen because it gives the same result every time.

03 / FOR YOUR BUSINESS

You may not need new software.

If your team already works in Google Drive, a shared inbox or a booking tool, you likely need one front door that puts the right thing in the right place.

What’s built—and what isn’t yet +

Not connected yet: the automatic mastering service and transcript search are planned. The desk says so on its own Connections page.

In preview: the vocal suite is on a preview version, not the live one.

Not built: there is no in-library player or client share link. Files open in Google Drive.

The build record: 38 deployments from October 21 to November 12, 2026, and 23 commits from November 9 to 12. That is a development window, not a measurement of full-time work.

Access: the workspace is private to the team, so there is no public link to it here.
