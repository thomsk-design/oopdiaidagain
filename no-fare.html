CASE STUDY / NO FARE! / IN THE NEWS

BUILT SATURDAY.
NEWS BY MONDAY.

Regina was about to lose its buses. People who ride them were facing a choice between a cab and groceries. On Saturday night I built No Fare!, a free carpool board that matches neighbours using the bus stops they already know. By Monday it was on the radio and in The Globe and Mail.

WHAT I BUILT

  • A ride board that speaks bus. Pick the stop you’d normally catch the bus at, where you need to go and roughly when. Drivers see people heading their way.
  • A live map. The city’s bus routes with offered rides parked on them, filtered by area.
  • Corners, not houses. Pick-ups and drop-offs are set at nearby corners. Home addresses never go on the board.
  • Messages without email addresses. Riders and drivers contact each other through the site. Nobody’s email is published.
  • Safety built in. A plain-language safety guide and rules, a report button, and posts that hide themselves after several reports.
  • Clean-up on its own. Every post is confirmed by email, comes with a take-down link, and expires after its date.
  • Free means free. No fares, no tips. It stays a favour between neighbours, not a ride service.

The trick wasn’t the code. It was using something every rider already understood: their bus stop.

HOW IT WENT

  1. SAT NOV 21, NIGHTThe buses are stopping Monday.I started building that evening. The first ride was posted at 11:02 pm.
  2. SUN NOV 22Shared everywhere.The link spread across Facebook. At first, more people were offering seats than asking for them.
  3. MON NOV 23, MORNINGOn the radio.980 CJME ran the story at 7:15 am. The lockout started at 11.
  4. MON NOV 23National.The Canadian Press story carried it to The Globe and Mail and Yahoo News Canada. SaskToday and 620 CKRM followed.
  5. MON NIGHT TO TUE NOV 24The best possible ending.The city and the union reached a tentative deal. Buses were back the next afternoon, and No Fare! could stand down.

WHAT I CARED ABOUT

  • Plain, warm words, like a notice on a bus shelter. No app to download, no account to make.
  • Privacy before features. If a choice meant showing where someone lives, it didn’t ship.
  • Not taking sides. The site was about getting people to work and school, not about the dispute.
  • Its own look: prairie-sky blue, wheat and a red route line, built around a map of the city’s bus routes.
  • Credit where it’s due: the footer says it was built by Oops I AI’d Again.

03 / FOR YOUR BUSINESS

Your “buses stopped” moment.

What the numbers mean +

Where they come from: the site’s own database, checked November 26, 2026. They count confirmed posts that were still up. Unconfirmed and removed posts aren’t included.

What they don’t say: No Fare! connects people. It doesn’t track whether a ride happened, so there’s no “rides completed” number here.

The coverage: linked above. It’s reporting, not an endorsement.

Under the hood: one web page, a few small server functions, a database I already had and email from an existing domain. No app store, no new subscriptions.
